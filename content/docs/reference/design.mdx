---
title: Design System
description: The visual language, semantic tokens, geometry, depth, typography, and motion of 4AF.
---

4AF feels like a set of precise instruments: quiet, compact, warm-neutral, and tactile. The
shell recedes so interactive states can be unmistakable without making every surface loud.
The system uses one amber accent as a deliberate signal rather than a default coat of paint.

## Visual atmosphere

- **Restrained and utilitarian.** Controls are compact, legible, and free of ornamental chrome.
- **Warm rather than clinical.** Neutral ramps carry a subtle warm hue instead of Tailwind's
  common blue-grey cast.
- **Tactile but controlled.** Press feedback, travelling indicators, and short entrances make
  controls feel physical without delaying work.
- **Layered by purpose.** Fill and elevation communicate whether something is sunk into the
  page, anchored to it, or floating above it.

## Color palette and roles

Components consume semantic roles, never raw palette values. A consuming project can reskin
the library by remapping those roles without rewriting component classes.

| Role | Light treatment | Dark treatment | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Background | Warm near-white (`oklch(0.985 0.002 70)`) | Neutral near-black (`oklch(0.1448 0 0)`) | Page ground and sunk wells |
| Surface | White (`oklch(1 0 0)`) | Soft black (`oklch(0.178 0 0)`) | Chrome and anchored popups |
| Raised surface | White (`oklch(1 0 0)`) | Deep black (`oklch(0.130 0 0)`) | Dialogs, sheets, palettes, and toasts |
| Foreground | Warm near-black (`oklch(0.155 0.006 70)`) | Warm near-white (`oklch(0.985 0.002 70)`) | Primary text and neutral actions |
| Focus ring | Amber (`oklch(0.702 0.176 52)`) | The same amber | Focus and deliberate emphasis |
| Destructive | Vivid red (`oklch(0.6 0.225 26)`) | The same red | Irreversible and failed actions |
| Success | Deep green (`oklch(0.52 0.13 152)`) | The same green | Confirmed outcomes only |

Primary is ink, not color. Amber is spent on focus and exceptional emphasis; destructive red
and success green only appear when their meanings are real. This keeps color informative.
Quiet destructive actions may use red text without adding a red hover surface; the danger signal
remains clear without turning a compact menu or toolbar row into a solid warning block.

A component's fill communicates its layer. `background` is the page and sunk wells such as
Input, Checkbox, Radio, and Sidebar Inset. `surface` is chrome and anchored popups such as
Sidebar, Dropdown Menu, Popover, and Hover Card. `surface-raised` belongs to elements floating
over everything: Dialog, Alert Dialog, Sheet, Command Palette, and Toast. A consuming project
decides what those semantic roles resolve to.

## Typography

Geist Sans is the interface voice: compact, neutral, and highly legible. Geist Mono is reserved
for code, command examples, keyboard hints, and small technical labels.

The scale is deliberately modest: 11px technical labels, 12px compact text, 13px default UI,
15px body copy, then 18px, 22px, 28px, and 36px headings. Display sizes tighten their leading
and tracking; body copy receives more air. Updating numbers opt into tabular figures so labels
around them do not shift.

## Geometry and shape

Corners range from a precise 3px rounding to a generous 28px container radius. Nested shapes
are concentric: the outer radius equals the inner radius plus the padding between them. A
14px shell with 4px of padding therefore contains a 10px child. Reusing one radius for both
creates visibly uneven corners.

Controls are compact by default. Icons remain geometrically centered inside their boxes;
leading and trailing icons beside text receive optical spacing through component attributes
rather than one-off margins.

Button-like controls share a 30px/34px/38px scale for `sm`/`md`/`lg`; component-specific objects
such as badges, checkboxes, radios, sliders, and table density keep their own geometry.

## Depth and elevation

Buttons and cards use a transparent `shadow-border`: a hairline ring, a close lift, and a soft
ambient shadow. It reads correctly over any background and avoids the boxed-in look of a hard
border. Raised and overlay surfaces increase separation progressively.

Dark mode replaces most drop-shadow depth with restrained luminous rings. Heavy shadows become
grey smudges on near-black, while a translucent edge preserves the layer boundary. Real borders
remain reserved for dividers and table cells, whose purpose is separation rather than elevation.

## Component styling

- **Buttons** use neutral ink as the ordinary primary action, compact geometry, optical icon
  spacing, and tactile press feedback. Joined buttons keep their outer box still so seams do not
  open during a press.
- **Fields** sit at the background layer and strengthen their ring on focus. Invalid state is
  semantic and accessible, not merely a red decoration.
- **Anchored panels** use the surface layer with a scale, blur, and fade entrance originating
  near the trigger.
- **Overlays** use the raised layer and stronger elevation. Their behavior and focus management
  come from Base UI.
- **Status UI** shares severity colors across alerts, badges, and outcomes so the same state
  reads consistently whether it is a sentence, label, or temporary notification.

## Layout principles

Whitespace is compact but not crowded. Related controls sit in close groups; sections receive
clearer separation than individual fields. Page shells keep navigation stable and give the
content region ownership of scrolling. Popups and overlays are positioned relative to the
control that summoned them, preserving spatial continuity.

## Motion language

Motion explains state and spatial relationships. CSS handles hover, focus, press, and simple
entrances. Motion is reserved for exit lifecycles, gestures, springs, and layout animation.
Transitions name the exact compositor-friendly properties they change.

Four duration roles—fast, base, slow, and slower—cover everything from focus feedback to sheets.
Reduced-motion preferences collapse CSS transitions to 1ms so lifecycle events still fire;
JavaScript motion uses zero-duration transitions. Decorative loops stop instead of accelerating
into a strobe.
